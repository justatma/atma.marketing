a new class of machine-driven editorial intelligence

ernest-faulkner
engine

not AI writing and editing. editorial judgment — from a machine with a human editor’s mindset.

Most AI writing tools rewrite first and justify later. Ernest-Faulkner does the opposite. It analyzes what a passage costs the reader, what the writer gains from that choice, argues for leaving it alone, and only recommends intervention when the evidence is strong enough.

1the problem

most AI writing tools are
built to rewrite your words.

You give them a paragraph. They make it shorter, smoother, clearer, or more polished. That sounds useful. But it creates a serious problem: the AI often starts writing the way it thinks good writing should sound.

what the rewrite does
It removes odd sentences.
It explains things that were meant to stay implied.
It shortens long sentences that were working.
It tones down anger.
It cleans up fragments.
It replaces unusual words with safer ones.
the result may be
easier to read.
it may also be
less interesting, less precise, less human — and less like you.
the idea it was built around
the AI should have to prove that something is wrong before it earns the right to change it.
That sounds simple. It changes almost everything.

7context

change the answer.

Ernest-Faulkner does not judge sentences in a vacuum. Consider one sentence.

“Mistakes were made.”
STATEMENT

a corporate statement after misconduct
agency problem.

A real editor understands that. The engine is being designed to understand it too — its 30 context-reversal tests check exactly this: the same or nearly identical words, a different context, and a different correct editorial decision.

The process, the safeguards, the rules, and the tests behind the engine. Ernest-Faulkner is in development: its test corpus already exists and is growing, and the final editorial intelligence is being built against it.

9the process

judgment and rewriting
are separate jobs.

Most AI writing tools mix these together. They see a possible problem and immediately start rewriting. Ernest-Faulkner separates them. The process is closer to this:

judgment
1
Observe the text.
What is actually happening?
2
Identify the possible reader cost.
What makes this harder, slower, less precise, or less effective?
3
Identify the possible gain.
What might the writer be getting in return?
4
Make the strongest case for leaving it alone.
5
Decide whether there is really a problem.
6
Decide whether intervention is justified.
7
Define exactly what may be changed.
the boundary
rewriting · a separate job
Only then can a revision system touch the prose.
that boundary matters.

It keeps the AI from turning every editorial observation into a rewrite.

11silence

Most software feels useful only when it produces output, so writing tools tend to generate lots of suggestions. Ernest-Faulkner treats silence as a valid editorial decision.
